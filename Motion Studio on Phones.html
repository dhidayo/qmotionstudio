Advice · 1 October 2026

Getting Motion Studio onto iPhone and Android

Easier than usual, because of how the app is already built. Most of what remains is product and business work rather than porting.

The short answer

  • It already runs on phones. Motion Studio is a web app that does everything on the device and works offline, and it is already installable as a home-screen app. Nothing has to be rewritten to reach phones.
  • The engine it needs now exists on iPhone. Export uses the browser's built-in video encoder (WebCodecs). Safari has had the video half since iOS 16.4 and the audio half since iOS 26, so an iPhone on iOS 26 has everything the export uses. Every browser and every app's web view on iPhone uses Safari's engine, so this one fact settles the question for both the web app and an App Store app.
  • Store listings are a wrapper, not a rebuild. The same code can ship to the App Store and Google Play inside a thin native shell called Capacitor.
  • The real work is elsewhere: a phone-sized way of editing, subscriptions and accounts, and proving export speed on real, older iPhones.

The order I recommend

  1. Put the web app on a real address days It needs a domain with HTTPS. This is also the only honest way to test on a phone: the video encoder only works on secure pages, so a phone pointed at the development computer over Wi-Fi will look broken when it is not. Run the device checks further down.
  2. Design the phone flow the biggest piece The current editor is a desktop tool built around a timeline. Most people on a phone will do five things: pick a template, add photos, change the words, preview, then export and share. Give phones that guided path and keep the timeline for tablets and desktop. This decides whether the app feels good on a phone far more than any technical choice does.
  3. Wrap it for the stores with Capacitor 4–8 weeks Add what a website cannot do well: share straight to Instagram, TikTok and LinkedIn, save to Photos, the native photo picker, and store subscriptions. Those native features are also what gets an app past Apple's rule against apps that are only a website.
  4. Add accounts and subscriptions alongside step 3 Covered in the next section.

I would not rebuild natively. It only pays off if real iPhones turn out to be too slow at exporting, and even then the cheaper fix is a small native export add-on inside the Capacitor app rather than a second codebase.

What subscribers need that v1 left out

The build spec deliberately keeps accounts, payments and servers out of version 1, and today "Pro" is a switch inside the app. Paying subscribers need three things the app does not have yet:

  • A record of who has paid that works across all their devices.
  • "Restore purchases" when they get a new phone.
  • Receipt checking, so a subscription cannot be faked.

A service such as RevenueCat handles all three for Apple, Google and web payments together. It is free to start and then takes roughly 1% of revenue. Photos and projects can stay on the device: only the subscription status has to be online, so the promise that nothing is uploaded still holds. That promise is a real selling point against the big editors and worth keeping.

If the iPhone app offers sign-in with Google or another account provider, Apple also requires a privacy-focused option such as Sign in with Apple.

What to test on real iPhones first

I could not test this from here. This Mac has Xcode's command-line tools but not Xcode itself, so there is no iPhone simulator, and a simulator would not show real memory and heat limits anyway. These checks need the oldest iPhone you want to support.

  • Export a 15-second 1080p video. Note how long it takes, whether the phone gets hot, and whether the page reloads. iOS closes web pages that use too much memory.
  • Switch to another app during an export. iOS pauses pages in the background, so the export will need to ask people to keep the app open.
  • Check that text in the exported video uses the right fonts.
  • Add HEIC photos straight from the camera roll.
  • Share the finished MP4 to Instagram from the share sheet. This works from a web app on both platforms already.
  • Add it to the Home Screen and open it again a week later. Safari can clear a website's saved data after seven days without a visit, but apps added to the Home Screen keep theirs. That is a good reason to nudge iPhone users to install it.

Decisions for you

  1. Web first, or stores at launch?I would go web first. It takes days rather than weeks and tests everything else.
  2. What exactly is Pro, and what does it cost?Monthly and yearly prices, and which templates, lengths and export sizes it unlocks.
  3. When does sign-in happen?At first launch, or only when someone subscribes. Later is friendlier and keeps the free app account-free.
